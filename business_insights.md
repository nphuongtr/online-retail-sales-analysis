# Business Insights – Online Retail II Analysis

## 1. Overall Business Performance

The analysis of the cleaned Online Retail II dataset shows that the business generated approximately **£20.97 million in revenue** from **40,077 invoices**, with more than **11.42 million items sold**.

The average order value (AOV) was approximately **£523.31**, indicating a substantial average revenue amount per invoice.

### Key Performance Indicators

| Metric | Result |
|---|---:|
| Total Revenue | £20,972,594.55 |
| Total Invoices | 40,077 |
| Total Items Sold | 11,420,287 |
| Average Order Value | £523.31 |

### Business Interpretation

The results indicate that the business achieved significant sales volume and revenue during the analysis period. The average order value provides a useful baseline for evaluating customer spending and identifying opportunities to increase revenue per transaction.

## 2. Monthly Sales Trends

The monthly sales analysis shows noticeable fluctuations in revenue throughout the analysis period. November recorded the highest monthly revenue in both 2010 and 2011.

### Key Findings

- **Highest Revenue Month:** November 2011, with £1,509,496.33.
- **Lowest Full-Month Revenue:** February 2011, with £523,631.89.
- **Year-over-Year Comparison:** November 2011 revenue was approximately 2.7% higher than November 2010.
- **Seasonal Pattern:** Revenue increased from September through November in both 2010 and 2011.

### Business Interpretation

The results suggest that sales performance strengthened toward the end of the year, particularly during September, October, and November. This pattern may indicate seasonal demand or increased customer purchasing activity before the holiday season.

The business could prepare for this period by increasing inventory availability, planning promotional campaigns, and ensuring sufficient operational capacity.

**Data Limitation:** December 2011 contains data only through December 9, 2011. Therefore, it represents an incomplete month and should not be directly compared with full months.

### Investigation of an Unusual Product Transaction

Further investigation of PAPER CRAFT, LITTLE BIRDIE revealed that all 80,995 units were recorded in a single invoice (Invoice 581483) on December 9, 2011.

The transaction involved customer 16446 in the United Kingdom, with a unit price of £2.08 and total revenue of £168,469.60.

This explains why the product ranked highly in both revenue and quantity despite appearing in only one invoice.

**Business Interpretation:** This transaction appears to represent an unusually large bulk purchase. Although it contributed significantly to revenue, it should not be treated as evidence of consistent or recurring customer demand. The business could investigate large-volume transactions separately when evaluating product performance and forecasting inventory requirements.

## 4. Customer Analysis

The customer data analysis examined customer identification, transaction records, and revenue generated by identifiable customers.

### Key Findings

- **Total Records:** 1,041,652.
- **Records with Customer IDs:** 805,531 (77.34%).
- **Records without Customer IDs:** 236,121 (22.66%).
- **Unique Identified Customers:** 5,878.
- **Revenue from Identified Customers:** £17,743,429.16, representing approximately 84.60% of total revenue.

### Business Interpretation

The results show that identifiable customers contributed approximately £17.74 million in revenue. However, 22.66% of sales records do not contain customer IDs, limiting the ability to connect all transactions to individual customers.

The business could improve customer identification during transactions to support more accurate customer segmentation, purchasing behavior analysis, targeted marketing, and customer retention strategies.

### Top 10 Customers by Revenue

The analysis identified the ten customers who generated the highest revenue.

### Key Findings

- **Highest Revenue Customer:** Customer 18102 generated £608,821.65 across 145 invoices.
- **Highest Purchasing Volume:** Customer 14646 purchased 367,193 items, generating £528,602.52.
- **Most Frequent Customer:** Customer 14911 placed 398 invoices, the highest among the top 10 customers.
- **Top 10 Customer Revenue:** £2,826,476.26.
- **Revenue Contribution:** The top 10 customers generated approximately 15.93% of revenue from identified customers.

### Business Interpretation

The results show that a small group of high-value customers contributes meaningfully to overall revenue. However, customer value varies depending on spending, purchasing volume, and order frequency.

The business could focus on retaining high-value customers through personalized promotions, loyalty programs, and relationship management. It could also encourage repeat purchases from customers who currently place fewer orders.

### Customer Purchase Frequency Analysis

Customers were grouped into three segments based on the number of invoices: one-time customers, repeat customers, and frequent customers.

### Key Findings

- **Frequent Customers:** 1,801 customers (30.64%) generated £14,226,580.02, representing 80.18% of identified-customer revenue.
- **Repeat Customers:** 2,454 customers (41.75%) generated £2,948,675.31, representing 16.62% of revenue.
- **One-Time Customers:** 1,623 customers (27.61%) generated £568,173.83, representing 3.20% of revenue.

### Business Interpretation

The results show that frequent customers are the primary revenue contributors, generating more than 80% of revenue from identified customers despite representing approximately 30.64% of the customer base.

This indicates that customer retention and repeat purchasing are important to business performance. The business could prioritize high-value customer retention through loyalty programs, personalized promotions, and exclusive offers. It could also encourage one-time customers to make additional purchases to increase their long-term value.

## 5. Geographic Analysis

The geographic analysis examined revenue, invoice volume, and items sold across 43 country labels in the dataset.

### Key Findings

- **Largest Market:** The United Kingdom generated £17,870,977.76, representing approximately 85.21% of total revenue.
- **Invoice Contribution:** The UK accounted for 36,535 invoices, approximately 91.16% of all invoices.
- **Other Major Markets:** EIRE, the Netherlands, Germany, and France were the next four largest markets by revenue.
- **International Reach:** The business recorded sales across multiple international markets.

### Business Interpretation

The results show that the business depends heavily on the United Kingdom market, which contributes more than 85% of total revenue. Although the business reaches international customers, revenue remains highly concentrated in the UK.

This creates an opportunity to expand international sales, particularly in established markets such as EIRE, the Netherlands, Germany, and France.

The business could explore targeted international marketing, shipping improvements, and market-specific promotions to diversify revenue sources and reduce dependence on a single market.

## 6. Product Revenue Contribution

This analysis examined how much of the business's total revenue was generated by its top 10 highest-revenue product and transaction entries.

### Key Findings

- **Top 10 Revenue:** The top 10 entries generated £2,120,275.62 in revenue.
- **Revenue Contribution:** These entries accounted for approximately 10.11% of total revenue.
- **Revenue Distribution:** Approximately 89.89% of revenue came from all other entries.
- **Product Ranking Consideration:** The top 10 list includes service-related entries such as Manual, DOTCOM POSTAGE, and POSTAGE, not only physical merchandise.

### Business Interpretation

The top 10 entries contributed approximately 10.11% of total revenue, indicating that overall revenue was not dominated by these entries alone. The business generates revenue across a broader range of products and transaction types.

The business should continue monitoring its highest-revenue merchandise to understand customer demand and maintain product availability. Service-related entries should be evaluated separately from physical products to provide a clearer picture of merchandise performance.

## 7. Business Recommendations and Conclusion

### Business Recommendations

Based on the analysis, the following recommendations could help improve business performance:

1. **Diversify Geographic Revenue:** The United Kingdom generates approximately 85.34% of total revenue. The business should explore opportunities to expand in established international markets such as EIRE, the Netherlands, Germany, and France to reduce its dependence on the UK market.

2. **Strengthen Customer Retention:** Frequent customers generate approximately 80.18% of revenue from identified customers. The business should focus on retaining these customers through loyalty programs, personalized promotions, and incentives for repeat purchases.

3. **Improve Customer Data Collection:** Approximately 22.66% of transaction records do not contain customer IDs. Improving customer information collection would support more accurate customer segmentation, purchasing behavior analysis, and retention strategies.

4. **Monitor Product Performance:** The business should regularly evaluate its highest-revenue and highest-volume products to maintain appropriate inventory levels. Service-related transactions should be analyzed separately from physical merchandise.

5. **Prepare for Seasonal Demand:** Sales revenue generally increased toward November in both 2010 and 2011. The business should consider preparing inventory and marketing campaigns ahead of this period to take advantage of potential seasonal demand.
